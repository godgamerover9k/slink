Controls

Click a segment between two dots to draw a line.

Shift-click (or right-click) marks it ×.

Ctrl-click a square to fill it , Alt-click to fill it .

Repeating the same click removes the mark. Drag to repeat it across the puzzle.

Draw one closed loop. Each number says how many of its four sides the loop uses.

Stuck? Branch off and guess. Your first mark is the branch's premise, circled on the board.

Branch again inside a branch to go deeper, or from the puzzle for a rival guess.

Hit a contradiction and rule it out: the branch goes, and the opposite of its premise is written one level up.

CREDITS

Standing on other people's work

Slitherlink was devised by Nikoli, the Japanese puzzle publisher that also gave the world Sudoku.

Krazydad — Jim Bumgardner's puzzle collection. The drawing scheme here follows his: click a segment for a line, click again to take it away, drag to keep going.

Jonathan Olson's Slitherlink — two ideas are borrowed from it: reading the board by line weight rather than × marks, and claiming that two squares sit on the same or opposite sides of the loop without saying which is which.

Everything else — the shared puzzles, the branches, the generator and its solver — was built for this.

Made with an AI. This program — the page, the shared puzzles, the branches, the generator and its solver — was written by Claude, an AI assistant, working from a person's instructions. Expect the odd rough edge.
